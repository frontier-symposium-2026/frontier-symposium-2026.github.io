P01

Multiscale Molecular Dynamics Simulations of Polymer-Plasticizer Solid-State Electrolytes

MinJun Jung
MolCube, Inc.
Abstract
Multiscale molecular dynamics simulations were used to examine seven polymer-plasticizer solid-state electrolyte formulations. Formulation films were assembled and dried with Martini 3 coarse-grained models, backmapped to all-atom systems, hydrated with 150 mM NaCl, and simulated under a 1.6 V potential drop. Na⁺ diffusion, accessible free volume, and head-group coordination were associated with measured current density, while plasticizer water exposure and intermolecular organization were associated with cell viability, providing a molecular interpretation of the experimental screening trends.
P02

LNPDB: Structure-Function Dataset of Lipid Nanoparticles to Advance Data-Driven Design for Nucleic Acid Delivery

Geon Ko
molcube
Abstract
Lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) are widely used for nucleic acid delivery, but formulation and lipid data remain fragmented across the literature. We developed LNPDB, an integrated database that standardizes LNP formulations, experimental readouts, ionizable lipid structures, and CHARMM force field files for molecular dynamics simulations. LNPDB supports property- and structure-based searches and is continuously expanding with newly reported datasets. Future developments will further integrate AI-based prediction and molecular simulation-derived descriptors to advance data-driven LNP design.

P06

Crossing the Bacterial Barrier: Integrating Enhanced Sampling and Experiments to Decode Antimicrobial Peptide Action

Piotr Chyży, Joanna Trylska
Centre of New Technologies, University of Warsaw, Poland
Abstract
Understanding the membrane-disrupting mechanisms of antimicrobial peptides is key to discovering new antibacterials. We conducted Umbrella Sampling and Gaussian Accelerated Molecular Dynamics to map free-energy landscapes of peptide insertion and envelope disintegration using all-atom Gram-negative (including LPS) and Gram-positive (with a peptidoglycan mesh) membranes. To validate our simulations, we synthesized the linear peptide and its hydrocarbon-stapled analogs, evaluating their secondary structures and bactericidal potential. This framework elucidates complex peptide-envelope interactions, providing guiding the design of potent antibacterials. P08

λ-Walking: Reactive Molecular Dynamics with Continuous Charge Transfer from Bulk Water to Interfaces and Nonaqueous Electrolytes

Sangmin Lee
Seoul National University
Abstract
We present λ-walking, a reactive molecular dynamics framework that enables continuous charge transfer using the local electrostatic energy difference between proton donor and acceptor states. The method describes proton hopping associated with O–H bond breaking and formation while retaining an efficient empirical force field description. After validation in bulk water, λ-walking captures slower proton hopping at various aqueous interfaces. We further extend the framework to nonaqueous electrolytes, demonstrating its applicability to reactive charge transport across diverse solvation environments.
P09

Evaluating Amber Force Fields for Twist-Bend Coupling in DNA Minicircles

Yeonho Song1,2, Jun Soo Kim1,2
1 Department of Chemistry and Nanoscience, Ewha Womans University, Seoul 03760, Republic of Korea
2 Institute for Multiscale Matter and Systems, Ewha Womans University, Seoul 03760, Republic of Korea
Abstract
Molecular dynamics force fields are widely used to model sharply bent DNA, but their accuracy for sequence-dependent twist–bend coupling remains unclear. We evaluate three Amber-based force fields (parmbsc1, OL21, OL24) combined with TIP3P and OPC water in 92 bp DNA minicircles against PDB-derived nucleosome references. All six combinations underestimate coupling, though relative sequence trends are broadly reproduced, with CG/CG, GC/GC, and AG/CT as notable exceptions. OPC water substantially improves OL24, an effect linked to suppression of Type I kinks that otherwise distort minicircle geometry under TIP3P water.
P10

Molecular dynamics of Mff-membrane system

Hongren Xie1,Yi Wang1
1 The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Hong Kong
Abstract
Mitochondrial fission requires recruitment of dynamin-related protein 1 (DRP1) from the cytosol to the outer mitochondrial membrane (OMM), but how its receptor, mitochondrial fission factor (Mff), acts with the membrane though its transmembrane domain (TMD) remains unclear. We used all-atom MD to discover how the Mff transmembrane domain (TMD) engages mixed-lipid bilayers with or without cardiolipins (CL). The TMD remains stably inserted in all systems, showing only mild tilt and axial fluctuations. CL does not measurably alter bilayer thickness or integrity. Anchoring is supported by hydrogen bonds between R341/R342 and CL headgroups, with W337 further stabilizing the TMD. These findings reveal a robust insertion mode and pinpoint residues that likely enable Mff-driven DRP1 recruitment during mitochondrial fission.
P11

Modeling Coupled Ion Uptake and Charge Formation at Soft Bioelectronic Interfaces

Seonghyeon Kang1, Kyeong-jun Jenong2, and Chang Yun Son1
1 Department of Chemistry, Seoul National University, Korea
2 Department of Chemistry, Kyeonggi University, Korea
Abstract
Electrochemical doping at soft interfaces arises from concerted ion uptake, electronic charge formation, and polymer reorganization, but is difficult to represent in conventional molecular simulations. We develop an iterative framework that couples field-driven molecular dynamics with ion-exchange and charge-formation updates until steady state is reached. Applied to aqueous conjugated polymer–electrolyte interfaces, the simulations reveal how ion hydration influences the coupled doping process. This approach provides an atomistic model of coupled ionic and electronic processes in soft bioelectronic materials.

P13

Δ-ML Corrected Physics-Based Water and Ion Model for Efficient Many-Body Molecular Dynamics

Bae, Sangwon1, Jeong, Kyeongjun2, Son, Chang Yun1
1 Department of Chemistry, Seoul National University, Korea
2 Department of Chemistry, Kyeonggi University, Korea
Abstract
We present a physics-based force field for water and aqueous ions combining a SAPT-based pairwise baseline with a Δ-machine-learning correction for many-body interactions. Trained only on CCSD(T) dimer and trimer interaction energies, the model reproduces water hexamer energies and bulk liquid properties, and extends naturally to ionic solutions. By capturing many-body effects at a fraction of the computational cost of potentials such as MB-pol, our model enables accurate molecular dynamics simulations at the nanometer scale in aqueous and ion-containing environments.
P14

Physically Guided Generative Sampling of G-Quadruplex Transition Pathways

Juhwa Lee1, Seungwon Jeong2, Chang Yun Son2*
1 Department of Chemistry, Pohang University of Science and Technology, Korea
2 Department of Chemistry, Seoul National University, Korea
Abstract
Beyond static structure prediction, understanding conformational changes between metastable states requires continuous, chemically meaningful transition pathways. Using G-quadruplex DNA as a challenging system, we use metadynamics and clustering to identify representative intermediates, then connect them with TPS-DPS, a CV-free generative path-sampling framework. TPS-DPS training incorporates a hydrogen-bond-guided loss, while generated pathways are refined by the string method. Together, these elements extend the framework beyond static structures toward physically grounded, time-ordered transition mechanisms and suggest a transferable strategy for diverse biomolecules.
P15

Hybrid Enhanced Sampling for Lipid Flip-Flop Free-Energy Calculations

Junku Kang
Seoul National University
Abstract
Lipid flip-flop free-energy calculations commonly use the membrane-normal position as a reaction coordinate, but slow orthogonal degrees of freedom can remain poorly sampled near the bilayer center. In PSM translocation simulations, upper- and lower-derived configurations remained segregated despite replica exchange, with restrained simulations also showing persistent orientation differences. To improve sampling in this central region, we combine positional umbrella restraints with metadynamics along hidden collective variables, enabling more robust transition sampling and reliable lipid flip-flop free-energy profiles.
P16

Confinement-Induced Anisotropy in the Collective Behavior of Water

Chaemin Jeong1, and Chang Yun Son1
1 Department of Chemistry, Seoul National University, Korea
Abstract
Water is essential for biological function, yet in living systems it often experiences anisotropic environments created by interfaces and nanoscale confinement. Using MD simulations, we show that confinement reduces the dielectric constant as anisotropic positive dipolar correlations suppress collective polarization along the confined direction. The collective character also emerges in lateral transport, where correlated displacements generate cooperative motion among neighboring water molecules. Together, orientational and translational correlations provide a molecular picture of hydration and transport in heterogeneous biological systems.

Seungwon Jeong
Seoul National University
Abstract
Rechargeable lithium-ion battery performance, including cyclability and thermal stability, is strongly governed by local solvation environments and atomistic ion–ion and ion–solvent interactions within electrolytes. P18

Simulation Study of Chiral Supramolecular Polymer Networks

Yein Lim
Seoul National University
Abstract
Supramolecular stickers drive polymer network assembly through physical crosslinking, yet their influence on network topology has not been extensively investigated. We use coarse-grained molecular dynamics simulations to investigate how sticker chirality affects network topology and mechanical properties. In homochiral networks, helical stacking imposes a unidirectional rotational bias on polymer chains, promoting entanglements. These networks thus exhibit higher entanglement density and toughness than racemic networks. These results highlight sticker chirality as a design parameter for tough supramolecular polymer networks.

pH-Dependent Conformational Properties of Poly(alkylacrylic acid)s in Good and Poor Solvents

Seungbin Hong
Seoul National University
Abstract
Poly(alkylacrylic acid)s, including poly(acrylic acid) (PAA) and poly(methacrylic acid) (PMA), are ionizable polymers used in diverse applications. To elucidate their pH-dependent conformational behavior, we performed explicit-solvent MD simulations across a range of ionization levels under good and poor solvent conditions. PAA typically shows an approximately linear increase in apparent pKₐ with α′, whereas PMA displays a plateau near α′ ≈ 0.2, suggesting resistance to initial ionization and distinct underlying molecular mechanisms.
P20

Modeling Isomerization-Induced Changes in Morphology and Chain Relaxation of Azobenzene-Containing Polymers

Jaehyuk Lee1, Seonghyeon Kang1, Chang Yun Son1
1 Department of Chemistry, Seoul National University, Korea
Abstract
Azobenzene photoisomerization involves electronic excitation, which classical molecular dynamics (MD) simulations cannot describe explicitly. We therefore applied torsional biasing to mimic the photoisomerization-induced conformational changes and examine their effects on polymer morphology and chain relaxation. For polymers containing azobenzene units in the backbone, we induced trans-to-cis conversion by gradually modifying azobenzene’s C-N=N-C torsional potential. The resulting cis configurations exhibited looser packing and reduced parallel alignment of aromatic rings, accompanied by faster chain relaxation than their trans counterparts.

Abstract
Peptide–graphene biosensors use probe peptides to recognize odorants. However, the molecular basis of their selectivity remains unclear. P22

Influence of the Lipid Membrane Environment on Ion Channel Conformational Stability

Kohsei Hagishita1, Eiji Yamamoto1
1 Faculty of Science and Technology, Keio University, Kanagawa, Japan
Abstract
Potassium ion channels are membrane proteins that selectively conduct K⁺ ions across cell membranes. Recent single-molecule measurements have revealed that membrane thickness can modulate ion channel conductance. Here, we used enhanced sampling molecular dynamics simulations to characterize the free energy landscape of a potassium ion channel in lipid membranes of different thicknesses. Our results show that thicker membranes stabilize the closed conformation, whereas thinner membranes favor the open conformation. These results suggest that membrane thickness regulates ion channel function by altering its conformational stability.
P23

Physics-Guided Modeling of the Helicobacter pylori VacA Transmembrane Channel

Yitao Gou1, Yi Wang1
1 Department of Physics, The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Hong Kong SAR, China
Abstract
VacA, a major Helicobacter pylori virulence factor, forms an anion-selective hexameric membrane channel whose atomic structure remains unresolved. We developed a hierarchical modeling workflow combining AlphaFold-derived helices, symmetry, packing constraints and MD simulations. The resulting channel model is structurally stable, water-permeable, and reproduces experimentally observed mutational effects and chloride-over-sodium selectivity. Simulations further predicted proton conduction, which was supported by liposome-based proton transport assays. This work demonstrates the strength of an integrated, simulation-driven strategy for modeling membrane-inserted pore-forming toxins.

Viruses continuously evolve through mutations that can alter viral fitness and antigenicity. P25

Plane-Specific Ice Growth under Tenebrio molitor Antifreeze Protein: Pinning, Curvature, and Growth Anisotropy

Inyong Ji1, Chang Yun Son1
1 Department of Chemistry, Seoul National University, Korea
Abstract
Hyperactive antifreeze proteins bind several ice planes, but how adsorption interacts with plane-specific growth kinetics remains unclear. Using molecular dynamics of Tenebrio molitor antifreeze protein on basal and primary prism ice, we compare protein-free and protein-bound growth fronts. The intrinsic growth-rate anisotropy between the planes is strongly reduced on the protein-bound face, where the prism front develops larger curvature than the basal front. We test whether curvature and pinning account for this convergence, aiming at quantitative design criteria for ice-binding materials.
P26

Beyond the Interface: How C-tail Phosphorylation Modulates EGFR Variant Dimer Dynamics

Shweta Kumari
RIKEN, Kobe
Abstract
The EGFR kinase domain's C-terminal tail docks onto the kinase domain surface, but the range of kinase conformations this contact co-occurs with is unclear. We use multiscale molecular dynamics (Martini 3+Gō, all-atom CHARMM36m, CALVADOS3) and unsupervised clustering on salt-bridge, R-spine, activation-loop, and αC-helix markers to map the full conformational landscape — active-like, inactive-like, and intermediate states — sampled alongside C-tail-kinase domain contact. We identify force-field-specific limitations in this sampling, and outline a framework for extending this classification to C-tail phosphorylation and oncogenic mutation variants.
P27

Curvature Elasticity Governs Size-Dependent Band-Like Domain Organization in Phase-Separating Nanoscale Vesicles

Gayoung Kim1,2, Jun Soo Kim1,2,3
1 Department of Chemistry and Nanoscience, Ewha Womans University, Republic of Korea
2 Institute for Multiscale Matter and Systems, Ewha Womans University, Republic of Korea
3 Inflammation-Cancer Microenvironment Research Center, College of Medicine, Ewha Womans University, Republic of Korea
Abstract
In multicomponent membranes, liquid-ordered and liquid-disordered domains organize through competition between line energy and curvature elasticity; how this balance shifts at the nanoscale remains unclear. Using coarse-grained molecular dynamics of 30 nm DIPC/DPPC/CHOL vesicles and Helfrich analysis, we show this balance inverts at the nanoscale: liquid-ordered/liquid-disordered demixing drives solvent efflux and relaxation into an oblate shape, with a continuous liquid-disordered equatorial band and liquid-ordered polar caps. Curvature elasticity favors this band over a two-domain state below ~65 nm, establishing a size-dependent principle for nanoscale membrane organization.
P28

Linking Conformational Dynamics to Calcium-Dependent Activation through Physics-Based Evaluation of Enzyme Variants

Tianjie Li1, Yi Wang1
1 Department of Physics, The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Hong Kong SAR, China
Abstract
Understanding how metal binding regulates enzyme function requires connecting conformational dynamics with bioactivity. We combine molecular dynamics simulations, RMSD-based conformational scoring, and enzyme assays to investigate calcium activation of a fungal decarboxylase PcncAAAD using a series of simulation-guided mutants. Calcium binding at site A stabilizes a lid–rim structure, preserving the substrate-binding environment, whereas binding at site B stabilizes the C-terminal domain. Simulation-guided mutagenesis and experimental validation link structural stability to catalytic activity, providing mechanistic insights for engineering metal-activated enzymes.
P29

Collective Water-Motion Fluctuations within Aquaporin-4 Pores: A Molecular Dynamics Study

Naonobu Kuribayashi
Graduate School of Science and Technology, Keio University
Abstract
Molecular dynamics simulations of aquaporin-4 (AQP4) embedded in a lipid membrane were performed to investigate temporal fluctuations in pore-water motion. We analyzed time series of the collective mean displacement of water molecules within the pore using power spectral density. Low-frequency enhancement was observed in a reference system without an ion concentration difference across the membrane. We discuss the characteristics of these fluctuations and consider how transmembrane ion concentration differences may affect pore-water dynamics and asymmetric water transport.
P30

Sampling and Characterization of Magnetosome-Associated Transmembrane Helix Structures in Lipid Bilayers

Manato Suzuki
Keio University
Abstract
Magnetosomes are membrane-bound magnetic organelles in magnetotactic bacteria and contain a variety of membrane-associated proteins. Although several of these proteins are predicted to contain transmembrane regions, their structures and stability within lipid bilayers remain poorly understood. Here, we investigated the stability of the putative transmembrane region of MamJ as a single helix in a lipid bilayer using molecular dynamics (MD) simulations. Replica Exchange with Solute Tempering 2 (REST2) enabled extensive conformational sampling and showed that the region stably adopts a transmembrane helical structure. Moreover, this approach revealed multiple helix conformations with distinct bending patterns, indicating conformational flexibility within the membrane.
P31

Mechanistic Insights into Mitochondrial Phosphatidic Acid Transport by Ups1-Mdm35

Melanie König
University of Heidelberg
Abstract
The Ups1–Mdm35 complex transports phosphatidic acid between mitochondrial membranes, supporting cardiolipin synthesis. Using all-atom molecular dynamics simulations, we identified three Ups1 membrane-binding modes and propose distinct roles for T2 in productive membrane recognition and T1 in lipid delivery. Extended simulations capture complete lipid release, with pocket opening involving the ω loop and α3 helix and membrane insertion preceding final dissociation. Targeted mutagenesis and lipid-transfer assays support these functional assignments. Our findings reveal how membrane orientation and conformational dynamics coordinate successive steps of mitochondrial lipid transport.
